The register of trials

A study that never enters a public register is harder to find, and harder to count.

Global · 19 August 2025

Type size
Type size

A clinical trial is a plan before it is a result. The plan – who is enrolled, what is compared, which outcome counts – can be filed in a public register before the first participant is seen. The World Health Organization keeps a portal that searches the registries meeting its standard, rather than a single national list. 1source 1

The largest of those registries is run by the United States National Library of Medicine. It is one book, not the whole shelf. A trial recorded there may also be recorded in another registry, and a trial recorded nowhere is missing from both the count and the later reading of what was asked. 2source 2

Journals that follow the medical editors’ recommendations treat registration as part of the record, not as a courtesy. The point for this atlas is narrow. A result that cannot be walked back to a registered question is a thinner source than one that can. 3source 3
