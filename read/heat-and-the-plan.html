Why the same heat kills more in some places

A heat wave is counted twice: by thermometers, and by the registers of deaths. Plans work on the gap between the two.

Global · 9 October 2026

Type size
Type size

In the first twenty days of August 2003, France recorded about 14,800 more deaths than expected, a rise of around 60 percent. Most of those who died were old, and many died alone at home. The heat was a weather event; the death count also measured how little had been prepared for it. 1source 1

France's answer was administrative. Health authorities and Météo-France built an operational warning system and made it part of a national plan of actions: alerts issued by area, lists of isolated older people kept by town halls, and instructions for care homes and hospitals. 1source 1

Ahmedabad, in western India, took the same route from a different starting point. A heat wave in May 2010 killed 1,344 residents. In 2013 the city launched a heat action plan: early warnings, training for health workers, drinking water and shade in public places, and later a cool roofs programme that lowers indoor temperatures in poor housing. The plan is credited with preventing nearly 2,300 premature deaths. 2source 2

Japan built its answer around a single number. Since 2021 a nationwide heatstroke alert is issued when the wet bulb globe temperature, an index that combines heat, humidity and sunlight, is forecast to reach 33. A stronger special alert, for a forecast of 35 or above, was added in 2024. There were 613 alerts in the first year and 1,722 in 2024. 3source 3 4source 4

Europe as a whole shows how far such plans still have to go. A study published in Nature Medicine estimated 61,672 heat-related deaths in 35 European countries between 30 May and 4 September 2022: 18,010 in Italy, 11,324 in Spain and 8,173 in Germany. Women died at a markedly higher rate than men. 5source 5 6source 6

The three plans differ in scale and in the tools they use, but they share one premise. Heat cannot be stopped at the border of a city; the number of deaths it causes can be changed by what is done on the second and third hot day.
