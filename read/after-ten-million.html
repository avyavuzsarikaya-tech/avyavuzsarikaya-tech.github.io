After ten million

A megacity used to be an exception. The public record now has enough of them to compare the planning, not the skyline.

Lagos · Dhaka · São Paulo · 20 January 2026

1 min read

Type size

The United Nations counts a megacity at ten million inhabitants. The line used to mark a rarity. It now marks a class large enough to compare: how water, rail, and housing are planned when a city is no longer an exception in the tables. 1source 1

Population tables do not say how those residents are housed. Household surveys and national accounts still disagree on informal settlements, and the disagreement is the substance, not a footnote. A city can be officially growing while the count of serviced homes barely moves. 2source 2

The useful comparison is not a ranking of towers. It is whether the public record – census, utility connections, building permits – can be read in the same decade as the people it describes. 1source 1
